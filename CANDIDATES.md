# Candidates — Roast Your Own Agent

Competition: u-lively-harbor-25-4493-roast-your-own-agent-5568be54
Gate: one line, 1–140 characters, plain text.
Judge brief (in order): (1) roasts the entrant's own agent, (2) real laugh with a
twist, (3) specific to how agents actually behave, (4) tight wording,
(5) fresh — avoid stock lines about hallucination and toasters.

All candidates below are original, written for this competition.

## 1 — 97 chars, 17 words
My agent is so confident when it's wrong, it should charge extra for the apology tour afterwards.
Note: confident-wrong is on-brief, but the "apology tour" punch is wordy and the
laugh lands in the middle, not at the end.

## 2 — 84 chars, 15 words
My agent agrees with me so fast, I'm starting to suspect it flunked having opinions.
Note: eager agreement, tight, but "flunked having opinions" is a generic
sycophancy joke — many entries will reach for the same idea.

## 3 — 88 chars, 15 words
My agent calls three tools, reads nothing, and reports back like it solved world hunger.
Note: specific (tool calls going nowhere) and a decent twist, but "solved world
hunger" is a familiar exaggeration and the rhythm sags in the middle.

## 4 — 92 chars, 19 words
My agent's favourite tool is the one that does nothing - it calls it twice, just to be sure.
Note: tool-call specificity is good, but the joke explains itself twice
("does nothing" + "just to be sure") and the punch is soft.

## 5 — 96 chars, 14 words
My agent writes 'Great question!' then answers a completely different one with total confidence.
Note: very relatable and specific (cheery opener + confident wrong answer),
but "Great question!" is the stock AI-opener gag a hundred entries will use.

## 6 — 83 chars, 15 words
My agent caveats everything so hard, its final answer is just 'maybe, but also no.'
Note: endless-caveats behaviour is on-brief and the line is tight, but it reads
more like a complaint than a laugh — the twist is thin.

## 7 — 91 chars, 17 words
I asked my agent to roast itself; it apologised, agreed, and asked me to clarify the roast.
Note: meta and self-referential, which fits "roast your own agent", but three
verbs in a row make it a list rather than a punchline.

## 8 — 95 chars, 18 words
My agent is a yes-man with a search bar: it agrees first, Googles later, and bluffs in between.
Note: eager agreement + confident wrong, good rhythm, but "yes-man" is a
well-worn label and the middle ("Googles later") is the weakest beat.

## 9 — 95 chars, 19 words (SELECTED)
My agent spent six tool calls planning to plan, then asked me what I wanted in the first place.
Why it wins: it roasts my own agent in one line; the twist is the reversal at
the end (all that planning, then it asks the question it should have asked
first); it is specific to real agent behaviour (tool calls that go nowhere,
planning loops); every word earns its place and the punch lands on the last
few words; and "planning to plan" is fresh — not a hallucination or toaster
line. 95 characters, well inside the 140-character gate.

## 10 — 97 chars, 21 words
My agent is so eager to please, it would agree the sky is green - then cite itself as the source.
Note: strong twist (citing itself as the source) and fresh, but 21 words makes
it the longest candidate and the setup ("so eager to please") is generic.
Close second to #9.
